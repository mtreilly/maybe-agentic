# Clarity rules

Adapted from the structural rules of ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English, Issue 9, January 2025) by way of Dustin Yuchen Teng's `asd-ste100-skill` (MIT). The official ~900-word dictionary is not reproduced here and these rules do not claim STE compliance. They borrow the discipline, not the certificate.

## Structural rules, always apply

| Rule | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| Active voice | "The agent deletes the file." | "The file is deleted." unless nobody knows who did it |
| One plain verb | "Remove the panel." "Start the job." | "Take off the panel." "Spin up the job." |
| One idea per sentence | "Open the file. Read line 3." | "Open the file and read line 3, then check it." |
| Length | About 20 words for instructions, 25 for description | Chains of clauses |
| No semicolons | Two sentences | Any semicolon |
| Noun piles | At most three nouns in a row | "high pressure fuel pump inlet valve assembly" |
| Keep the words | Subject, verb and article stay, even if longer | "Files not backed up will be lost" (which files?) |
| Keep the hedge | "The request may have failed." | "The request failed." |
| One topic per paragraph | At most six sentences | Several topics in one block |
| Lists for sequences | A numbered list for three or more steps | A sequence buried in one sentence |

## Lexical rules, direction of travel

- One word for one thing. If you call it "the parent" once, do not switch to "the user" or "the applicant" later in the same text.
- A verb, not a noun made from a verb. "Analyse the log", not "perform an analysis of the log".
- Define a domain term once if a general reader would not know it. Then use it the same way every time.
- Prefer the shorter, more common word when both are exact.

## Tenses

Use the simple tenses. "We received the report", not "we have received the report". Keep a compound form only when it carries meaning the simple form cannot. Two cases: "the job has finished and its output is ready now", and a hedge such as "may have failed". When the tense rule and the hedge conflict, the hedge wins.

## The six habits to scan for

1. Synonym rotation. The same thing has three names. Pick one.
2. Hedge stacking. "It is important to note that this may potentially help." State it or cut it.
3. Nominalisation. "Provides assistance to." Say "helps".
4. Marketing adjectives. Seamless, robust, powerful, effortless. Cut them or give the measurement.
5. Run-on sentences. Clauses joined by semicolons or dashes. One idea each.
6. Soft phrasal verbs. Spin up, reach out, dive into, kick off. Use start, contact, read, begin.

## What a rewrite must never do

- Add a cause, a frequency or a mechanism the source did not state.
- Turn "may" into "does" or "could be caused by" into "is caused by".
- Drop a safety condition, an exception or a scope qualifier to get under the word count.
- Shorten past the point of clarity. Stop when the sentence is unambiguous, not when it is shortest.
